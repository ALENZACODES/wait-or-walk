# Rider test script (not yet run)

No rider testing has been done. This is a short script John can run later with five to ten Toronto subway riders, in person or on a call. It takes about ten minutes per rider. Record answers in a table; do not change the page between riders.

## Before you start
- Use a phone with the live page open: https://alenzacodes.github.io/wait-or-walk/
- Say: "This is a planning aid, not advice. Please think aloud. There are no wrong answers."
- Note the rider's usual line and how often they ride (daily, weekly, rarely). Do not record names.

## The five questions
1. **First look (30 seconds).** Show the page with the worked example (Kipling, headed to Jane). Ask: "In your own words, what is this page telling you to do?" Record whether they say wait, switch, or are unsure, and how long it took.
2. **The number.** Point at the 23%. Ask: "What does 23% mean here?" Record whether they describe it as the chance a delay like this lasts 10 minutes or more (correct), or as the chance of their own wait, or something else.
3. **Already underway.** Tap "Delay already reached 5 min". Ask: "What changed, and would you do anything differently now?" Record whether they notice the number and the minutes left changed, and their choice.
4. **Plan B.** Tap the St George fire example headed to Dupont. Ask: "Would you take the 26 Dupont? What would make you not take it?" Record whether they mention catching the bus on time, the timetable estimate, or checking TTC alerts.
5. **Trust.** Ask: "On a scale of 1 to 5, how much would you trust this on a real delay, and why?" Then: "What one thing would you change?"

## Afterwards
- Count how many riders read question 2 correctly and how many could act within 30 seconds in question 1.
- Report the counts and quotes as they are, including the bad ones. Do not claim more than the sample shows.
